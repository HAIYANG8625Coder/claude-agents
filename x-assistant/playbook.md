# X playbook

What gets attention and follows on X. `/x-research` updates this from real popular posts; `x-drafter` reads it before writing. Edit freely.

## Hooks (first line decides everything)
- Identity in one line: "Programmer in Singapore. Learning ___ in public." Short labels make people remember who you are. (Seen: solo builders who open with a 4-word bio line get steady 1k+ likes.)
- Specific result: "I built ___ in ___ hours with ___. Here's what happened:"
- Contrarian one-liner: "If your ___ needs ___, you've reinvented ___." / "Unpopular opinion: you don't need ___." Short, quotable, a real opinion. Often the top-liked format in AI coding.
- "Most underrated" claim + steps: "___ is the most underrated skill in ___. 1. ... 2. ..." (Top evals post this run.)
- Honest curious question: "Can I ask a dumb question? ___ vs ___ — why?" Real observations get replies.
- Number list: "3 things I wish I knew before ___:" / "N ___ every ___ must know:"
- Result first, how second: "People keep asking how I get ___ to ___ like this. Here's how 🧵" with a video of the finished thing (4k likes, 2026-11). The demo shows the result in the first 2 seconds, not the process.
- Surprising true story: "Claude Code just found ___ in my ___." Something real that happened to you, told in one line (563k views, 2026-11).
- News question, same day: "<news in one line>. Are you switching or sticking with ___?" Text only, no image needed (341k views, 2026-11). Only for real, verified news.

## Formats that work for a small account
- **"Let's connect" posts**: "If you're building with ___, ___ or ___, say hi / drop what you're working on." Small accounts got 500–750 likes and 200+ replies with this; it's the fastest way to find your first followers. Use sparingly (once every few weeks), and genuinely reply to everyone.
- **Bookmarkable lists**: practical steps, tools or lessons (3–7 items, one line each). Bookmarks often outnumber likes on these: people save them.
- **Workflow reveal**: "Here's how I actually use ___ day to day:" A real setup (e.g. one agent per area + one coordinator) beats theory.
- **Build in public**: what you made, what broke, what you learned. Real screenshots beat words.
- **Before → after** (prompt, code, workflow).
- **Mini-threads** (3–5 posts) for anything that needs explaining; the first post must stand alone.
- **Questions** experts enjoy answering, e.g. "What's your eval setup for agents?"

## Reach for a new account (learned 2026-11-13 from Michael's own stats)
- Michael's posts on 2026-11-12/13 got 4–8 views each: with few real followers, X barely shows posts to anyone, so the writing isn't the bottleneck yet.
- The fix is distribution: about 10 genuine replies a day under bigger AI builders, a bio that says the theme, and a pinned best post. X Premium gives replies priority; it's Michael's call, never assume it.
- Write about what the reader gets, not about Michael's own setup. "My voice clone" is about him; "clone your voice from 2 minutes of audio, free plan" is for the reader.
- Stay on theme (AI agents, Claude Code, AI video). Off-theme posts (e.g. NVDA stock) got the fewest views and confuse who should follow.

## Why people follow
- One clear theme: they expect *more* of something useful. Michael's angle: "programmer in Singapore learning to build and control AI agents, sharing honestly".
- The most-followed accounts in these niches post the same kind of thing every day (one recurring identity + short takes). Consistency beats one viral post.
- Replies: thoughtful replies under bigger accounts in the same niche bring early followers faster than posts alone.
- Profile: bio + pinned post should say what you post about.

## Popular but NOT for Michael (works for reach, hurts trust)
A large share of top AI posts right now are hype bait. Recognize it; don't copy it:
- ALL-CAPS shock openers with swearing ("THIS IS F*CKING INSANE", "holy f*ck").
- Fear hooks: "You won't have a job in 2027 if you don't learn this."
- Magic-prompt promises: "Paste this into Claude tonight, it will change your life."
- "Comment X to get access" giveaways.
They get likes and bookmarks but attract followers who expect more hype, and they clash with `voice.md`. Michael's edge is being honest and specific.

## Never do (hurts trust or the account)
- Engagement bait: "Like if you agree", "Follow for more", fake giveaways.
- Invented results, numbers or experiences.
- Copying other people's posts or threads.
- More than 2 hashtags; tagging big accounts who aren't involved.
- Links in the main post when it can be avoided (reach drops; put them in a reply).
