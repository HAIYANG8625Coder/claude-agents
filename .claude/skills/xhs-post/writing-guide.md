# How to write a better Xiaohongshu post

Read this before writing (step 2) and use it again as the checklist in step 4. It covers content only. Format limits and publishing are in `SKILL.md`.

Most of these tips come from creator and marketing write-ups, not from Xiaohongshu itself, and the platform changes often. Treat the numbers as rough guides and trust the account's own data (views, saves, comments in the creator center) over any rule here.

## 1. Pick a topic worth posting
The topic decides about half of how a post does. Before writing anything, answer three questions in one line each:
- **Who is it for?** A specific reader, e.g. "parents whose child starts Primary 1 next year", not "everyone".
- **What problem does it solve, or what question does it answer?** If it's only "what I did today", it's a diary, not a post.
- **What will they take away?** Steps, a checklist, real numbers, a template, a decision, or an honest story they can learn from.

If you can't answer all three, change the angle before writing. A diary moment can often be turned into something useful: a ride photo becomes "a cycling route + distance + where to get water".

What tends to work:
- **Real experience with real stakes.** Something that actually happened to the author and that others are going through too (visa or PR, a school, moving, costs, a tool that broke).
- **Real numbers and details.** Prices, dates, timelines, steps, screenshots with private data hidden. Specific beats general every time.
- **Series.** Follow-ups to a story people already care about ("PR month 3 → month 6 → result") bring readers back.

What tends not to work: vague diary titles, photos with no story, and posts that only help the author (asking for tickets, asking for recommendations, selling something).

## 2. Search keywords
A large share of views comes from search, so write for it:
- Find the 1–2 phrases a reader would actually type (e.g. 新加坡小一, 新加坡PR, HDB开销, Claude Code教程). Use the Chinese term people search for, not only the English one. Some products have a common Chinese name besides the official one, e.g. 推特 for X: use the one readers search for in the title and the other in the body or a topic.
- Put the main keyword in the **title, ideally in the first 10–12 characters**, then again in the **first line of the body** and on the **cover**.
- Make the topics (话题) match the keywords: one big topic plus 2–4 niche ones.

## 3. Title (20 characters max)
- Lead with the keyword, then a specific promise.
- Numbers and questions help: "3件事", "9个坑", "真实开销", "被拒后怎么办？".
- Add a light personal or emotional touch if it fits (🥹, 踩坑, 真实), but the promise comes first.
- The title must match the content. No clickbait. A reader who feels tricked won't save or follow.
- Patterns: `<keyword>｜<number>个<thing>`, `<keyword>真实<thing>｜<year>版`, `<situation>？<number>件事先做好`.
- Never: a date-only or mood-only title (随笔1015, 新加坡, 梦里与现实). Nobody searches for those.

## 4. Cover (the first image)
People see only the cover and title in the feed and must tap to see more, so the cover does most of the work.
- Big text, few words (one hook line plus an optional short subtitle), high contrast, readable at thumbnail size.
- Make it look like an answer to a search: the cover repeats the keyword and the promise.
- A real photo with text on it often feels more trustworthy than a polished ad look. Clear, original images only. Avoid blurry, heavily filtered or reposted pictures.
- Keep a consistent style across posts so followers recognise the account.

## 5. Body
- **First line = hook.** Who I am plus why this matters to the reader, with the keyword in it. Show what they'll get ("9个坑都整理好了").
- **Make it worth saving.** Saves count for a lot. Checklists, step-by-step guides, cost breakdowns, templates and summary cards get saved. A short "建议先收藏⭐" is fine when the post really is reference material.
- **Scannable.** Short lines, one idea per line, numbered steps or ✅ items, a blank line between sections.
- **True and specific.** Real numbers, real steps, real outcomes. No invented facts.
- **End with a real question** the reader can answer from their own life ("你家也是明年上小一吗？最担心孩子哪方面？"). Not just "欢迎评论". Comments and follows count more than likes.

## 6. Images after the cover
- Card 2 is usually the overview or summary card (the one people save).
- Then one card per step or point, and photos that prove it's real.
- Hide anything private: names, IDs, faces of other people's children, addresses, tokens.

## 7. After publishing
- Reply to comments in the first 1–2 hours. Early engagement helps decide how far a post spreads.
- If a post does well, plan the follow-up. If it flops, change the title or angle next time, not just the photos.
- Post on a regular rhythm (e.g. 2–3 times a week at similar times) rather than in bursts.
